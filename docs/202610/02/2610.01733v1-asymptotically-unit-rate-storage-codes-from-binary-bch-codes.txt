ASYMPTOTICALLY UNIT-RATE STORAGE CODES FROM BINARY BCH CODES 

ARYEH LEV ZABOKRITSKIY (YOHANANOV) 

Abstract. A storage code on a graph assigns a symbol to each vertex so that the symbol can be recovered from its neighbors. We prove that the binary full-parity storage codes on triangle-free coset graphs of primitive BCH codes have rate tending to one for every fixed error-correction capability of at least two. The conclusion also holds for an increasing error budget under an explicit growth condition. The proof bounds the binary rank of convolution matrices associated with polynomial maps. Low coordinate degree forces cancellation in the polynomial representing the image, and a polynomial rank bound converts this cancellation into a quantitative estimate for the storage rate. For the double-error-correcting family over 

F2m , we prove that the storage deficiency is Θ(((1 + √5) /4) m). This refinement follows from an exact Fibonacci formula for a polynomial coefficient rank and a matching exponential lower bound after finite-field evaluation. 

1. Introduction 

A storage code on a graph is a collection of assignments of symbols to the vertices in which each symbol is determined by the symbols at neighboring vertices. The graph prescribes the available recovery sets, and the objective is to maximize the rate of the code. Complete graphs support rates tending to one through a single parity check. Requiring the graph to be triangle-free removes this construction and leads to a substantially different question. Barg and Zémor [2] studied binary full-parity storage codes on coset graphs of linear codes and constructed families with rates above one half. They also identified BCH and Reed– Muller codes as natural sources of further examples. Constructions yielding asymptotically unit-rate binary storage codes on triangle-free graphs were obtained by Golovnev and Ha-viv [6], Barg, Schwartz, and Yohananov [1], and Huang and Xiang [8]. Deng, Huang, Weng, and Xiang [4] proved that the family arising from the double-error-correcting BCH code has asymptotically unit rate, and studied further constructions with two coordinates over an extension field. Here we consider the primitive binary BCH code with designed distance 2 s + 1, whose parity-check columns can be written as (a, a 3, . . . , a 2s−1), a ∈ F∗

The argument uses the Boolean algebraic degree of the parity-check columns. We establish a rank bound for convolution by the image of a polynomial map over F2 and apply it to the BCH power map. The key step is that low coordinate degrees force high-degree coefficients to vanish in the polynomial recording the parity of the fibres. The subsequent rank step is the polynomial rank argument of Croot, Lev, and Pach [3]. The relevant dimensions explain the scope of the application: for fixed s, the BCH syndrome space has dimension at most 

sm , whereas the nonconstant quadratic evaluation map has target dimension m(m + 1) /2. For the double-error-correcting family we also determine the exponential order of the rank. The proof computes an exact Fibonacci formula for the coefficient rank of the polynomial used in [4], and derives matching exponential upper and lower bounds after evaluation over F2m . This sharpens the displayed rank estimate in that work, whose exponential base is 2 + √2 before normalization. A related open problem comes from the quadratic Reed–Muller construction proposed by Barg and Zémor [2, author version, Section 7.2, item 3], who left the determination of its storage-code rates open. We do not settle whether these rates tend to one. Instead, we identify the corresponding Cayley graph with the rank-one graph on symmetric binary matrices and express the unit-rate question as a binary matrix-rank problem in that setting. We also give an equivalent formulation through the intersection of the graph’s cut and cycle spaces. These connections offer further approaches to the Reed–Muller problem: rank and dimension estimates in these settings give storage-rate bounds, while storage-code estimates constrain the same algebraic and graph-theoretic objects. We first describe the storage construction and its convolution matrix. We then prove the polynomial rank bound and apply it to the BCH family. The double-error-correcting case receives a sharper analysis through its coefficient spaces. The final section gives these reformulations for the quadratic Reed–Muller family. 2. The full-parity storage codes obtained from primitive binary BCH codes have asymptot-ically unit rate for every fixed error budget of at least two. A single bound for polynomial maps proves this statement and also permits an error budget that grows with the extension degree. The bound depends on the Boolean coordinate degree and the dimension of the syn-drome space, so it can be applied to other parametrized connection sets with these quantities under control. For the double-error-correcting family, the intersection of coefficient spaces gives an exact Fibonacci rank formula. The accompanying finite-field lower bound determines the expo-nential order of the storage deficiency, refining the qualitative unit-rate conclusion in this case. Determining the largest growth range of the BCH error budget that still gives asymp-totically unit rate remains an open direction. For quadratic Reed–Muller evaluation, the rank-one graph on symmetric binary matrices gives an exact formulation of the correspond-ing question. In that family the target dimension is quadratic in the input dimension, and a stronger rank argument is needed to reach asymptotically unit rate.
